The six folds

Open a fold, do the thing, mark it competent. Twelve PDU per fold. No one is checking.

  1. Spread a full bath towel flat on the bed, long edge facing you. Smooth every crease outward from the centre — folds set poorly on a rumpled base and the whole bird lists to one side later.

    A hand towel will also fold, but it produces a duck. The duck is not examinable.

    Assessor's noteTerry towelling holds a crease. Bamboo and microfibre do not, and will slump within the hour.

    +12 PDU on completion

  2. Bring the far long edge in to the middle, then the near one, so the two edges meet along an imaginary centreline. You should end up with a long narrow band, roughly a third of the original width, with a seam running down its spine.

    The two halves must be equal. Everything from here is symmetrical, and an uneven band gives you one large wing and one small one.

    Assessor's notePress the folds down with a flat palm. Do not pinch — pinching stretches the pile.

    +12 PDU on completion

  3. At one end of the band, fold the two corners in to the centre seam so they meet — the same move as the nose of a paper plane. You now have an arrowhead about 20 cm long. This becomes the neck and head.

    Fold it tighter than feels necessary. A loose point cannot hold its own weight and the head will nod forward.

    Assessor's noteLeave the last few centimetres of the tip narrow. That is the beak.

    +12 PDU on completion

  4. Starting at the blunt end, roll each side tightly and evenly inward toward the centre seam. Work both rolls together, a few centimetres at a time, and stop where the arrowhead begins.

    Two matched rolls sitting side by side make the body. Tight rolls hold their shape; loose rolls unwind the moment the swan is lifted.

    Assessor's noteRoll on the bed, not in the air. The mattress gives you the counter-pressure.

    +12 PDU on completion

  5. Slide one hand underneath, keep the rolls pinned with the other, and turn the whole thing over so the rolls sit beneath and the smooth side faces up. Push the two rolls together until they touch, and the point lifts clear of the bed.

    Sit it upright. The rolls become the wings and the folded underside becomes the base — widen or narrow that base to set how upright the bird stands.

    Assessor's noteIf it topples, the rolls are uneven. Unroll the lighter side and redo it rather than propping the swan up.

    +12 PDU on completion

  6. Draw the point upward and curve it into a lazy S — back at the base, forward at the top. Pinch the final few centimetres flat and angle it down for the beak. Hold the curve for a slow count of ten so the fibres take it.

    Place the swan at the foot of the bed, square the wings, and step back to check it from the doorway. That is the angle it will be seen from.

    Assessor's noteOne folded flannel under the base hides the seam. Chocolates optional. Croissants are still not accepted.

    +12 PDU on completion
